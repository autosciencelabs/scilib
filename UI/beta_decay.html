Beta Decay as an Evidence Graph

Evidence graph of Scilib.BetaDecay, written in Lean 4

October 21, 2026

AbstractBeta decay initially presented a profound crisis for nuclear physics: classical two-body kinematics required emitted electrons to carry a sharp, single monoenergetic energy line. However, calorimetric measurements by Ellis and Wooster revealed a smooth, continuous energy spectrum. Wolfgang Pauli and Enrico Fermi resolved this conflict by introducing the neutrino as an invisible third body that shares decay energy. This page lays out the refutation of classical two-body decay alongside the support for Pauli-Fermi neutrino theory. Pick a world below, and hover or tap any box for details.

Figure 1: One row for the beta spectrum experiment. On the left is what the classical two-body model predicts. The red dashed line indicates a direct contradiction with the observed continuous spectrum. The Pauli-Fermi neutrino model underneath accounts for the continuous energy observation. The experiment is on the right, with its verdict for each world in words. Small coloured dots show which worlds include a node: blue for classical two-body, teal for neutrino theory.
