Bernoulli’s Principle as an Evidence Graph

Evidence graph of Scilib.Bernoulli, written in Lean 4

October 21, 2026

AbstractBernoulli’s principle says that where a fluid moves faster, its pressure is lower. It works very well for slow, smooth, frictionless flow, and engineers use it every day. But it was stretched too far in three places: air moving at a good fraction of the speed of sound, wings tilted past their stall angle, and water draining from a tank. This page lays out, test by test, what the plain (“ideal”) version predicts, what experiments saw, and what the fuller “real flow” models say. Pick a world below, and hover or tap any box for its full statement.

1 The evidence graph

Figure 1: One row for each experiment. On the left is what the ideal version of Bernoulli’s principle predicts. The red dashed line means that prediction and the observation below it cannot both be true. The real-flow model underneath explains the observation. The experiment is on the right, with its verdict for each world in words. Small coloured dots on a box show which worlds include it: blue for ideal Bernoulli, teal for real flow. Faded boxes are not part of the world you selected.
